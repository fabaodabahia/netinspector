🎮 What is CGNAT and how do I know if I am behind it?

CGNAT (Carrier-Grade NAT) is a method used by ISPs to share a single public IPv4 address across dozens of residential customers simultaneously due to IPv4 exhaustion.

Behind CGNAT, your router receives an address in the reserved RFC 6598 range (100.64.0.0 to 100.127.255.255) on its WAN port while websites see the ISP shared IP. This breaks port forwarding and often causes Strict NAT (Type 3) in online games.

🎯 How does CGNAT affect Gaming, Cameras, and Remote Access?

P2P games and consoles (PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo Switch) rely on direct peer connections. Under Symmetric NAT in CGNAT, external ports change per destination, triggering Strict NAT warnings and party chat errors.

Home servers, self-hosted VPNs, and local CCTV/DVR cameras without cloud relays also become unreachable over IPv4.

🛠️ How to opt out of CGNAT or fix Strict NAT?

1. Check your router WAN IP: if it falls between 100.64.0.0 and 100.127.255.255, CGNAT is 100% confirmed.

2. Enable IPv6: if native IPv6 is active, it bypasses CGNAT completely. 3. Contact ISP Support: request a dynamic public IPv4 (CGNAT opt-out) citing remote CCTV/DVR access or work VPN requirements.

❓ CGNAT & Gaming NAT Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the official CGNAT IP range?

RFC 6598 reserves 100.64.0.0/10 (100.64.0.0 to 100.127.255.255) specifically for Carrier-Grade NAT.

Does CGNAT slow down download speeds?

For streaming and browsing, speed is mostly unaffected. However, P2P gaming and port-dependent services may experience Strict NAT and matchmaking issues.

Can I ask my ISP to remove me from CGNAT?

Yes. Many ISPs allow free opt-out to a dynamic public IPv4 when customers need port forwarding for security cameras or remote work VPNs.

If IPv6 is active, do I still need to leave CGNAT?

Modern IPv6-ready apps work without CGNAT, though legacy IPv4-only games and servers still benefit from a public IPv4.
