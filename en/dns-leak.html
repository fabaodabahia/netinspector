🛡️ What is a DNS Leak?

When connected to a VPN, all network traffic and DNS queries should route through an encrypted tunnel directly to your VPN provider DNS servers. A DNS leak happens when your device bypasses this tunnel, sending requests to your local internet provider (ISP) DNS servers instead.

This means that despite believing you are anonymous, your ISP can still monitor which websites you visit, entirely undermining your VPN privacy.

⚠️ The Danger of WebRTC Leaks

WebRTC is built into modern browsers for peer-to-peer real-time communication. However, STUN requests can bypass VPN network adapters, exposing your true public IPv4 or IPv6 address directly to websites.

What happens if I disable WebRTC?

Disabling WebRTC increases privacy by preventing real IP leaks, but real-time audio/video services may stop working.

⚠️ What will stop working

  • 📹 Browser video calls (Google Meet, Zoom Web, Discord Web, WhatsApp Web)
  • 📤 Web P2P file sharing (Snapdrop, ShareDrop, WebTorrent)
  • 🎮 Cloud gaming and interactive browser streaming

✅ What will NOT be affected

  • 💻 Dedicated desktop/mobile apps (Zoom, Discord, Teams, WhatsApp)
  • 📺 Standard video streaming (YouTube, Netflix, Twitch)
  • 🌐 General web browsing (websites, social media, online stores)

💡 Recommendation: instead of disabling completely, use an anti-leak extension (such as uBlock Origin) that keeps WebRTC functional without exposing your real IP.

🔒 How DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH) & DoT Protect You

Standard DNS queries travel unencrypted over UDP port 53, making them vulnerable to surveillance. DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH) and DNS-over-TLS (DoT) wrap requests in secure TLS tunnels, preventing tampering and snooping.

❓ VPN & DNS Privacy Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does my VPN always hide my DNS queries?

Not always. Misconfigured VPN clients or operating system fallbacks can route lookups to your local ISP router.

How can a website discover my real IP even with an active VPN?

Mainly through WebRTC leaks or unprotected IPv6 traffic. If your VPN only handles IPv4, your true IPv6 might be exposed.

What are DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH) and DNS-over-TLS (DoT)?

They are modern security protocols encrypting DNS traffic, preventing eavesdropping on public Wi-Fi and ISP monitoring.

How do I fix a DNS leak detected by this test?

Enable DNS Leak Protection and Kill Switch inside your VPN app, or manually set trusted DNS servers like Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) on your network adapter.

Should I completely disable WebRTC?

Disabling it completely breaks browser meetings (Meet, Zoom, WhatsApp Web). Instead, use extensions like uBlock Origin that prevent IP leaks without disrupting services.
