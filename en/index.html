🌐 What is a Public IP Address?

The IP (Internet Protocol) address is the global numerical identifier that specifies your gateway to the world wide web. Just like a physical postal address allows mail delivery to your home, a public IP address enables servers, streaming platforms, and apps to deliver data packets back to you accurately and safely.

Two versions coexist on the modern Internet: IPv4, composed of 4 dot-separated numbers (e.g., 177.136.244.12), and IPv6, a 128-bit hexadecimal standard (e.g., 2804:14d:5483::1). NET INSPECTOR checks both instantaneously.

🏢 How does NET INSPECTOR detect your ISP?

Global IP address blocks are allocated by regional registries (such as ARIN, RIPE NCC, and LACNIC) to telecommunications carriers and corporations known as Autonomous Systems.

When inspecting your public IP, NET INSPECTOR queries public ASN databases and BGP tables, accurately showing your provider name and Autonomous System Number.

📍 Why can IP Geolocation be Inaccurate?

Users are often surprised when the reported city does not match their exact home. IP geolocation does not use GPS; it maps your IP to your ISP nearest routing center or Point of Presence (PoP).

Mobile cellular connections (4G/5G) or CGNAT networks frequently pool residential customers across broader regional hubs.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is my IP address static or dynamic?

Most residential broadband connections use dynamic IP addresses that cycle periodically or upon modem reboot. Fixed static IPs are usually reserved for business subscriptions and dedicated servers.

What is the real difference between IPv4 and IPv6?

IPv4 supports about 4.3 billion unique addresses, which have been exhausted globally. IPv6 features 128-bit addresses, offering practically unlimited addresses without NAT overhead.

How can I hide or mask my public IP address?

You can use a trusted VPN, the Tor network, or encrypted proxies. Our IP inspection card also runs a WebRTC leak check to make sure your VPN is not leaking your true IP.

Can anyone find my home address from my IP?

No. Third parties can only see regional estimates (city and ISP). Only your Internet service provider maintains legal subscription records matching IPs to physical addresses.
